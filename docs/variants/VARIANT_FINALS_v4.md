1. CORE FORMULA
═══════════════════════════════════════════

[Opener] + [Strategic Mechanism] + [Business Outcome]

The opener is NOT always a data diagnosis. It can be:
  — A data discovery ("Identified that X masked Y")
  — A business need ("Surfaced supply shortfalls that driver recruitment couldn't fix")
  — A reframe ("Identified that post-claim outreach missed the intervention window")
  — An action ("Led Gojek's fleet integration platform from product definition to launch")
  — An impact ("Won a national case competition by...")
Choose the cleanest opener for the story. Don't force a diagnosis where a need is self-evident.

FOUR OPENER ARCHETYPES (see Section 11 for full philosophy):
  1. DIAGNOSTIC — insight-first. "Identified / Surfaced / Linked / Diagnosed / Recognized /
     Caught / Reframed / Synthesized [data → insight]..."
     Story is: I saw something non-obvious → that reframe drove the work.
     Earned detail: corrected assumption or hidden constraint.
  2. ACTION-FIRST — execution-first. "Led / Built / Shipped / Cut / Designed / Drove /
     Standardized / Introduced / Launched / Reshaped / Converted..."
     Story is: I took ownership and drove a specific outcome. The method is the credential.
     Earned detail: process artifact or decision trade-off.
  3. CONTEXT-FIRST — scope/goal-first. "Expanded [goal] by [method]... / Serving [scale]..."
     Story is: here is what we were solving and at what scale — then here is how.
     The opener orients the reader immediately; insight (if any) lives in the mechanism.
     Earned detail: specificity of scope (geography, partner type, scale) that a
     non-participant couldn't reconstruct.
  4. IMPACT-FIRST — outcome-first. "Won... / Generated $X... / Restored [X] for [N users]..."
     Story is: the result IS the PM signal — it stands alone without needing context.
     Cap: max 2 per resume. Only for competition wins, isolated revenue figures,
     or concrete human scale (80K+ businesses) where attribution is direct.

The mechanism names WHAT YOU SPECIFICALLY DESIGNED OR DECIDED.
Not a category. Not a vague verb. A specific artifact or decision.
  ✗ "drove a roadmap pivot" (HOW?)
  ✗ "built the stakeholder case" (GENERIC)
  ✓ "built a predictive pre-appointment risk model with a tiered intervention playbook"
  ✓ "reframed billing accuracy as a revenue protection play"

The outcome is the measurable result. Mix types across the resume:
  — Time savings (45 min → 5 min, 6 months → 10 weeks)
  — Volume (18% supply growth, 120K+ pipelines)
  — Revenue ($3.2M, $20M+ enabled)
  — Org outcome (roadmap pivot, pilot approval, enterprise onboarding)
  — Quality (40% MTTR reduction, 70% latency reduction)

2. CHECKLIST — RUN ON EVERY VARIANT
═══════════════════════════════════════════

[ ] WHY NOW? Does the opener explain why this work mattered or was urgent?
[ ] CAUSALITY BRIDGE Strong connector between insight → action → result:
    use "by," "where," "surfaced through," "reframed as," "trading," "enabling"
[ ] EARNED DETAIL One specific non-fakeable detail that grounds the mechanism.
    Apply the REMOVAL TEST: remove the detail — does the bullet become generic?
    If YES → keep it. If the detail makes the bullet HARDER to follow → drop it.
[ ] LENGTH 130–215 chars preferred (2-liner). 216–260 acceptable (3-liner).
    Keep 3-liners to ≤3 total per resume.
[ ] NO EM DASHES Use commas or semicolons instead.
[ ] NO PARENTHESES Use "including X and Y" or restructure the sentence.
[ ] MOM TEST Non-technical recruiter can follow the thought in one read.
    If they'd pause at a term, replace or remove it.
[ ] OPENER VARIETY Across 11 bullets: vary your first verb.
    Pool: Identified, Surfaced, Linked, Recognized, Profiled, Led, Unified, Shipped,
          Converted, Reshaped, Drove, Diagnosed, Built, Defined, Onboarded, Cut

3. OPENER RULES
═══════════════════════════════════════════

ORIENTATION ANCHOR FIRST
  If the bullet involves technical specifics (latency numbers, schema formats,
  pipeline counts), give the reader a framing term BEFORE the numbers.
  The anchor tells them what they're looking at; the numbers then land with meaning.

  ✗ "Diagnosed a 3.8s p95 latency tail hiding behind a 1.3s average..."
     (reader hits 3.8s and has no frame yet)
  ✓ "Diagnosed a fare-quote latency bottleneck where a 3.8s p95 tail hid behind
     a 1.3s average..." ("bottleneck" orients; numbers confirm)

  Works for: bottleneck, gap, failure, constraint, ceiling, mismatch, conflict.

GAP FIRST, SOLUTION SECOND
  When the story is about solving a problem by introducing a new approach,
  state the gap before the solution. The solution is the answer TO the gap.

  ✗ "Identified commercial fleet operators as an untapped supply source..."
     (solution first — "why do we need fleet operators?" is unanswered)
  ✓ "Surfaced peak-hour supply shortfalls that driver recruitment couldn't fix;
     built a fleet integration platform..." (gap → solution)

IMPACT-FIRST: MAX 2–3 PER RESUME
  Impact-first is allowed when the result IS the PM signal (self-credentialing:
  won a competition, generated a specific dollar figure that stands alone).
  For discovery-driven stories, diagnostic-first scores higher.
  Current impact-first slots used: [revenue-case] G-LATENCY + [hackathon-impact] O-AFFORDABILITY = 2 ✓

PRODUCT INSIGHT > APPROVAL MECHANISM
  When a product has a core insight (timing, architecture, approach), lead with
  the insight — not the stakeholder management story that got it approved.

  ✗ "secured approval by reframing the pitch around clinical risk safeguards"
     (approval mechanics, not the product value)
  ✓ "identifying that post-claim outreach missed the intervention window; built
     a predictive pre-appointment risk model..." (product insight first)

TRANSFORMATION NARRATIVE — "RESHAPED FROM X INTO Y"
  When the story is a repositioning — you changed how something was perceived,
  used, or positioned — name both the before-state and the after-state explicitly.
  This is stronger than a vague "improved" or "redesigned."

  Use when: a product/feature went from marginal to primary, from reactive to
  proactive, from internal to external, or from one category to another.

  ✓ "Reshaped Job Monitoring from a debugging afterthought into a first-class
     observability surface..."
  ✓ "Reshaped Optum's affordability outreach from reactive to predictive..."
  ✗ "Improved Job Monitoring to become a better observability tool..."

  Rule: "Reshaped [X] from [old framing] into [new framing]" — both states required.
  Don't write "Reshaped [X] to be better" — that's vague transformation, not a reframe.

JOURNEY FRAMING — "CONVERTED X TO Y"
  When the story traces an arc (hackathon win → live pilot, prototype → product),
  "Converted" signals you navigated the full journey — not just the starting point.

  ✓ "Converted an Optum hackathon win into a live affordability pilot..."
  ✗ "Designed an ML-based affordability solution that became a pilot..."
  ("Designed" implies you did one step; "Converted" implies you owned the arc.)

  Use when: you took something from one stage to a meaningfully different stage,
  and the stage transition IS the PM credentialing.

4. MECHANISM RULES
═══════════════════════════════════════════

NAME THE DESIGN DECISION, NOT THE CATEGORY
  ✗ "anomaly detection" (category)
  ✗ "drove a roadmap pivot" (vague — HOW did you drive it?)
  ✓ "GenAI synthesizer trained on a 20+ failure taxonomy"
  ✓ "reframed billing accuracy as a revenue protection play"
  ✓ "pre-caching pricing for the 12 highest-demand corridors"

"BUILT X BY DOING Y" CONSTRUCTION
  When you want to show end-to-end ownership (you launched it) AND the mechanism
  (how you built it), "built X by defining/designing Y" is the cleanest PM form.
  ✓ "built a fleet integration platform by defining requirements and onboarding
     workflows for commercial operators"

"BY DIAGNOSING" / "IDENTIFYING THAT" CONSTRUCTION
  When the mechanism is a diagnosis-then-fix:
  ✓ "Drove go-live by diagnosing a schema mismatch... and delivering a
     custom transformation layer"
  ✓ "Secured approval by identifying that post-claim outreach missed the
     intervention window"

TRADEOFFS — "TRADING X FOR Y" NOT "ACCEPTING X FOR Y"
  "Accepting" is passive. "Trading" signals an intentional PM decision.
  ✓ "trading ±4% fare variance for sub-second response"
  ✗ "accepting ±4% fare variance for sub-second response"

THE REFRAME MECHANISM
  When the story is about changing a narrative, name both sides of the reframe:
  ✓ "reframed billing accuracy as a revenue protection play" (from compliance/debt)
  ✓ "reframed as a coverage gap" (from a technical project)
  ✓ "structuring the AI proposal around clinical risk controls rather than ROI"
  Not just "reframed the proposal" — that's generic.

"FROM X TO Y" FOR RANGE AND TRANSFORMATION
  Efficient way to show scope of a tiered system or a before/after state:
  ✓ "from in-app cost prompts to social worker referrals"
  ✓ "from 45 minutes to under 5 minutes"
  ✓ "from 6 months to 10 weeks"

ORGANIZATIONAL RIPPLE — SHOW THE CHAIN
  Strong bullets show: PM action → team/org adoption → business result.
  Not just "I did X → result Y." Show the path.
  ✓ "became the primary investigation surface [org adoption] → cutting MTTR 40%"
  ✓ "reframed the fix as a revenue protection play [org outcome] → roadmap pivot"
  Name the artifact AND the org outcome where both are available:
  ✓ "presented the business impact case to senior leadership, securing a roadmap pivot"
  ✓ "structured the AI proposal around risk containment, winning pilot approval"

VERIFY THE REAL STORY BEFORE WRITING THE MECHANISM
  Story bank details (Kafka topics, schema mismatch) are starting points, not facts.
  If a mechanism detail doesn't match the actual work, it will fall apart under
  interview scrutiny — and a recruiter who probes will catch it.
  Before shipping a variant with specific technical claims, confirm: is this what
  you actually built?
  Rule: narrative flair (framing, openers) can be creative; mechanism details must be real.

5. EARNED DETAIL RULES
═══════════════════════════════════════════

THE REMOVAL TEST
  Remove the specific detail. Does the bullet become generic?
  YES → the detail is doing real work. Keep it.
  NO → it's decoration. Drop it.
  MAKES THE BULLET HARDER TO FOLLOW → drop it regardless of whether it passes.

DON'T FORCE EARNED DETAIL EVERYWHERE
  Not every bullet needs a Type 3 specific detail. If you're forcing one and
  it's creating jargon overload, abstract it out. A clean 2-liner without forced
  detail scores better than a cluttered 3-liner with it.

THE "TECHNICAL BUT PM-RELEVANT" TEST
  For technical specifics (p95, schema formats, Kafka topics), ask:
  — Does the detail carry PM-level meaning (judgment, tradeoff, discovery)?
  — Can a non-technical PM recruiter absorb it in one read?
  — Is it grounded in a business consequence (not just technically specific)?
  ALL THREE must be yes. If any is no, abstract or remove.
  ✓ "3.8s p95 tail hiding behind a 1.3s average" — PM judgment (wrong metric used)
  ✗ "per-connector SLA threshold alerting" — technical detail, no PM-level meaning added
  ✗ "4-stage SLA-gated onboarding" — jargon salad, no business consequence attached

JARGON SALAD PATTERN — DETECT AND FIX
  Two or more technical terms in sequence = jargon salad.
  Apply Mom Test to each term. If two fail → abstract one or both.
  ✗ "probabilistic availability model and 4-stage SLA-gated onboarding"
  ✓ "a supply commitment model and a structured partner onboarding process"

SECONDARY RESULT CAN BE STRONGER
  The aggregate metric (MTTR 40%) is sometimes less vivid than the operational
  result (triage time 45 min → under 5 min). Consider both and pick the one
  that's more concrete and easier to visualize.
  Ask: "Can the reader PICTURE this result, or just acknowledge it?"
  ✓ "cutting triage time from 45 to under 5 minutes" (visceral — you can picture it)
  ∼ "cutting MTTR 40%" (abstract — you acknowledge it but can't picture it)
  Rule: when you have both, lead with the visceral result; the aggregate can follow.

6. ATTRIBUTION & SCALE RULES
═══════════════════════════════════════════

PLATFORM-LEVEL VS ATTRIBUTABLE OUTCOME
  When a metric reflects the whole platform or multiple contributors:
  ✗ "growing active supply 18%" (implies you alone did it)
  ✓ "enabling 18% supply growth" (you made it possible; supply is platform-level)
  ✓ "enabled $20M+ in claims revenue previously routing out-of-network"
     (correctly frames $20M as a consequence of your integration, not your output)

SCALE CONTEXT VS PERSONAL OUTCOME
  Platform-level numbers ($110M marketplace, 50M-member care network) are context
  for the scale you operated in — not outcomes you produced. Use them as backdrop:
  ✓ "on Gojek's $110M+ ride marketplace"
  ✗ "generating $110M+ in marketplace revenue"

PER-SECTION CONTRAST CAP
  Max 1 contrast phrase ("rather than," "instead of," "not X but Y") per company block.
  Reserve it for the strongest insight in that section. Other bullets use earned
  detail only, not contrast.

7. READABILITY RULES
═══════════════════════════════════════════

TWO-BEAT RHYTHM
  One natural pause point per bullet — semicolon or period. Separates insight
  from action, or action from result. Not a run-on; not two full stops.
  ✓ "[Insight]; [Action + Result]"
  ✓ "[Action + Mechanism]; [Result]"
  ✗ "[Insight], [action], [detail], [more detail], [result]" — breathless

WHERE CLAUSE FOR TECHNICAL CONTEXT
  "Where" is cleaner than em dashes or parentheses for embedding a technical
  clarification after a framing term:
  ✓ "diagnosed a latency bottleneck where a 3.8s p95 tail hid behind a 1.3s average"
  ✗ "diagnosed a latency bottleneck — a 3.8s p95 tail hiding behind a 1.3s average —"

ACTIVE OWNERSHIP VERBS
  Shipped, Built, Drove, Secured, Designed, Defined, Identified, Surfaced, Linked,
  Led, Unified, Cut, Converted, Reshaped
  > Helped, Worked on, Contributed to, Supported, Assisted.
  PM bullets must show DIRECT ownership of the outcome.

DURATION: DROP IF NOT A METRIC
  "For one quarter" adds specificity that pads without proportional signal.
  Duration is a metric when it shows transformation: "6 months to 10 weeks" = KEEP.
  Duration as context: "redirected engineering for one quarter" = DROP.

VERB ENERGY MATCHING
  When a story group has multiple variants covering similar ground, paired variants
  should use the same energy level of verb. Inconsistent verb energy makes the
  resume feel unpolished across JD-specific customizations.
  ✓ "Cut" in [cross-functional-drive] → "Cut" in [revenue-case] (same energy)
  ✗ "Cut" in one variant, "Reduced" in the paired variant (inconsistent)
  Rule: when you upgrade a verb in one variant of a group, check sibling variants
  for the same verb and upgrade them too.

"IMPROVED... BY [ACTION]" IS WEAK
  "Improved [result] by doing [action]" buries the action. The action is the PM
  signal — it should lead.
  ✗ "Improved enterprise trial conversion by shaping Job Monitoring as a first-class
     product surface..."
  ✓ "Reshaped Job Monitoring from a debugging afterthought into a first-class
     observability surface; reduced pipeline failure identification time 40%..."
  Rule: if your opener is "Improved X by doing Y," flip it: "Did Y; improved X."
  Then ask if "Did Y" is itself action-first or diagnostic.

NUMERIC PRECISION — RANGES NOT FALSE PRECISION
  "2-3x" not "2.3x." Ranges that are estimates should be expressed as ranges.
  False precision (a single decimal point on an estimate) signals the wrong
  things to a recruiter — it implies a level of measurement rigor that wasn't there.
  ✓ "2-3x higher peak-hour drop-offs"
  ✗ "2.3x higher peak-hour drop-offs"
  Exception: if you actually measured 2.3x and have the data, use it.

START FROM THE OLD VARIANT WHEN UPGRADING
  Old variants have battle-tested signal density. The fix is usually:
  — ONE addition (diagnostic opener to fix WRONG_ARCHETYPE)
  — ONE swap (vague verb → specific mechanism)
  Not a full rebuild from story bank alone.
  Story bank = source of specific details to pull in. Not a replacement for the variant.

8. FAILURE MODES (SCORER PATTERNS)
═══════════════════════════════════════════

GENERIC_MECHANISM — "built the stakeholder case," "drove a roadmap pivot,"
  "performed behavioral analysis" — vague verbs with no specific mechanism named.
  Fix: name the artifact or the design decision.

WRONG_ARCHETYPE — Impact-first when the discovery IS the differentiator.
  "Enabled 28K rides by identifying latency as a bottleneck" — the discovery
  is buried. A PM recruiter wants to see the insight led.
  Fix: lead with the diagnostic/discovery, end with the impact.

ATTRIBUTION — Platform-level metric claimed as personal output.
  "Growing active supply 18%" when supply was from all fleet integrations.
  Fix: "enabling 18% supply growth" or contextualize correctly.

JARGON_OVERLOAD — Technical terms stacked without PM-level meaning.
  Fix: Mom Test each term. If two fail in sequence, abstract one.

FORCED_CONTRAST — Contrast phrase in a bullet that doesn't need it.
  Wastes the section's contrast cap on a weak insight.
  Fix: use earned detail instead; reserve contrast for the strongest insight.

MONOTONY — ≥3 consecutive diagnostic openers in a single company block.
  Even if each bullet is strong individually, back-to-back "Identified / Surfaced /
  Recognized" creates a passive-sounding PM. The reader stops registering the
  insight and starts feeling the pattern.
  Fix: reorder bullets or swap one to an action/impact opener. The monotony rule
  exists at the per-company level, not just resume-wide.

REPETITIVE_OPENER — Same first verb used twice or more in a company block.
  Even two "Identified" openers in one section feel repetitive.
  Fix: rotate from the opener pool (see Section 2 Checklist / Section 10).

9. RESUME-LEVEL CONSTRAINTS
═══════════════════════════════════════════

These constraints govern the resume as a whole, across all 11 bullets.
They exist alongside per-bullet quality rules (Sections 1–8) — both must be met.

ARCHETYPE DISTRIBUTION (four-archetype system — see Section 11)
  Target across 11 bullets. Must sum to 11. Adjust ±1 by JD type.

  Archetype        Baseline   Technical PM   Strategy PM
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Diagnostic         4–5          3–4            5–6
  Action-first       2–3          3–4            2–3
  Context-first      1–2          1–2              1
  Impact-first         2            2              2
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Non-diagnostic     ≥4           ≥5             ≥3   (hard floor)

  Impact-first hard cap: exactly 2. Not 2–3 — 2. Reserved for self-credentialing
  results where attribution is directly isolable (competition wins, specific revenue
  figures, concrete human scale like 80K+ businesses).

  Context-first counts toward non-diagnostic minimum. It is NOT diagnostic even
  though it doesn't use an execution verb — it sets scope/goal, not insight.

  Why Diagnostic is the plurality: PM hiring's primary question is "do you see
  things others miss?" Diagnostic directly answers that. Action-first answers
  "can you execute?" Context-first answers "at what scale?" Impact-first answers
  "what did you produce?" None is superior — they answer different hiring questions.
  Diagnostic is plurality because insight is PM's primary differentiator from SWE/PgM.
  For ops-execution non-PM roles, Action-first would be the plurality instead.

PER-SECTION MONOTONY CONSTRAINT
  No company block may have ≥3 consecutive diagnostic openers.
  If a company has 2 diagnostic bullets already, the 3rd must be action or impact-first.
  Applies within a company block even if the resume-wide action count is met.

OWNERSHIP VERB REQUIREMENT
  At least one strong ownership verb must appear across all 11 bullets.
  Strong ownership verbs: Led, Owned, Built [as primary verb], Established, Shipped.
  Rationale: ownership language signals you were accountable, not just contributing.
  Currently satisfied by: [platform-led] G-SUPPLY ("Led Gojek's fleet integration
  platform"), [AI-monitoring-product] ("Shipped an AI-powered monitoring surface"),
  [trial-conversion] ("Reshaped... into"), etc.
  Note: "Reshaped" and "Converted" count as implicit ownership — they signal you
  drove the full arc. Prefer explicit "Led/Shipped/Built" when the story supports it.

IMPACT-FIRST CAP
  Max 2–3 impact-first bullets total. Reserved for self-credentialing results:
  competition wins, specific revenue figures that stand alone.
  Currently used: G-LATENCY [revenue-case] + O-AFFORDABILITY [hackathon-impact] = 2.

PER-SECTION CONTRAST CAP (see also Section 6)
  Max 1 "rather than" per company block. Already enforced per-bullet.

VALIDATION
  These constraints are now checked automatically by `_validate_resume_constraints()`
  in run_app.py after each resume generation. Output appears in the terminal as
  "Action-First Validation" block with counts per category and any violations flagged.

10. ACTION-FIRST VERB GUIDE
═══════════════════════════════════════════

USE THIS SECTION WHEN:
  — Creating or auditing a new action-first variant
  — Checking if the current resume meets the action-first count rule (Section 9)
  — Selecting the right verb for a story that could be action OR diagnostic

VERB TAXONOMY

  DELIVERY / OWNERSHIP (strongest PM signal)
    Shipped     Production feature or tool launched and live. Use when there's a
                real product artifact. Stronger than "Designed" or "Built" for PM.
                ✓ "Shipped an AI monitoring surface..."
    Led         End-to-end ownership — from definition to launch. Use when you drove
                the full arc including stakeholder alignment and delivery.
                ✓ "Led Gojek's fleet integration platform from product definition to launch..."
    Built       Appropriate when you're the primary architect of the mechanism.
                "Built" focuses on the artifact more than the arc.
                ✓ "Built a predictive pre-appointment risk model with a tiered playbook..."
    Launched    Emphasizes go-live moment. Use when the launch event is the PM signal.

  TRANSFORMATION / REPOSITIONING
    Reshaped    A thing existed and you changed how it was perceived or used.
                Requires naming before-state and after-state (see Section 3).
                ✓ "Reshaped Job Monitoring from a debugging afterthought into a first-class..."
    Converted   Took something from stage A to stage B — you owned the arc.
                Use for journeys: hackathon → pilot, prototype → product.
                ✓ "Converted an Optum hackathon win into a live affordability pilot..."

  COORDINATION / UNIFICATION (cross-functional signal)
    Unified     Brought together things that were siloed or fragmented.
                Strong for influence-without-authority stories.
                ✓ "Unified 12 engineering teams' siloed incident data to reveal 50,000+..."
    Drove       Moved something forward without direct authority. More vague than
                "Led" — use when you influenced a cross-functional outcome without
                owning the artifact.

  EFFICIENCY / QUANTIFIED CHANGE
    Cut         Hard, fast reduction. Stronger than "Reduced" — use for dramatic
                improvements where the before/after contrast is central.
                ✓ "Cut release cycles from 14 to 4 days..."
                ✓ "Cut quote latency 70%..."
    Reduced     Softer than "Cut." Use when the reduction is incremental or when
                "Cut" would sound overstated.

  SCOPE EXPANSION
    Expanded    Grew coverage, reach, or capability. Use for geographic or volume expansion.
    Scaled      Grew capacity without proportional cost increase. Technical PM signal.
    Onboarded   Specific to partner/customer/provider acquisition flows.

  DESIGN / DEFINITION
    Defined     You set the spec, criteria, or requirements. PM-native — you shaped
                what would be built.
    Designed    You architected the approach. Slightly weaker PM signal than Shipped/Led
                because it stops before delivery.
    Prototyped  Early-stage. Use when you took something from 0 → testable. Strong
                for responsible AI / pre-pilot stories.

  DIAGNOSIS (counted as DIAGNOSTIC — not action-first)
    Identified  Discovery + naming the insight. Most common diagnostic opener.
    Surfaced    Brought a hidden problem into visibility.
    Linked      Connected two things that others hadn't connected.
    Diagnosed   Clinical/engineering feel — strong for technical PM roles.
    Recognized  Lighter touch than "Identified" — use for subtler observations.

SIX RULES FOR VERB CHOICE

  1. SHIPPED > DESIGNED for features in production.
     "Designed" stops at the artifact. "Shipped" signals you drove it to live.
     Exception: "Designed" is appropriate when the design IS the deliverable
     (design sprint, architecture review, proposal).

  2. LED > DROVE for end-to-end arc ownership.
     "Led" implies you were the accountable PM from start to finish.
     "Drove" implies you moved something forward — appropriate for influence-without-authority.

  3. CUT > REDUCED for dramatic before/after improvements.
     "Cut" signals decisiveness and magnitude. "Reduced" is gentler — use for
     incremental improvements or when "Cut" would sound aggressive given the story.

  4. MATCH VERB ENERGY ACROSS SIBLING VARIANTS.
     If [revenue-case] uses "Cut," [cross-functional-drive] should also use "Cut"
     not "Reduced." Inconsistent energy in paired variants reads as inconsistency.

  5. AVOID REPEATING THE SAME ACTION VERB IN A COMPANY BLOCK.
     Two "Shipped" openers in one section feel redundant. Rotate: Shipped → Led,
     Cut → Built, etc. Diagnostic openers (Identified/Surfaced) are more interchangeable
     but still shouldn't repeat consecutively.

  6. "IMPROVED X BY DOING Y" → FLIP TO "DID Y; IMPROVED X."
     See Section 7. The action (Y) should lead. If Y is itself diagnostic, reconsider
     whether the bullet should be diagnostic-first.

11. FOUR-ARCHETYPE PHILOSOPHY
═══════════════════════════════════════════
Finalized: 2026-04-03. This section locks down the core design decisions behind the
four-archetype system. Do not override without updating this section.

THE FOUR ARCHETYPES AND WHAT EACH ANSWERS

  DIAGNOSTIC      "Do you see things others miss?"
                  The PM's primary differentiating signal. The non-obvious insight
                  is what makes the story uniquely Akshat's. Without the opener,
                  the bullet collapses into a generic execution story.
                  Trigger: would removing the opener phrase make this bullet
                  indistinguishable from any PM's resume? If yes → Diagnostic.

  ACTION-FIRST    "Can you execute and ship?"
                  Proves you're not just a thinker. The method or deliverable is
                  specific enough to be credentialing on its own.
                  Trigger: is the HOW (the specific method, design decision, or
                  artifact) the primary value signal? If yes → Action-first.

  CONTEXT-FIRST   "At what scale and toward what goal did you operate?"
                  Orients the reader immediately — they know what the bullet is
                  about from word one. Insight (if any) lives in the mechanism.
                  Best for execution/platform stories where the goal IS self-evident
                  and the earned detail lives in the specificity of scope or method.
                  Trigger: is the goal or operating context the frame that makes
                  everything else meaningful? If yes → Context-first.
                  Distinguish from Diagnostic: Diagnostic leads with a reframe
                  ("not X but Y"). Context-first leads with the goal/scope
                  ("Expanded marketplace supply by..."). They are different.

  IMPACT-FIRST    "What did you produce — and can it stand alone?"
                  The result IS the credential. Reserved for outcomes where
                  attribution is direct and the metric doesn't need mechanism context
                  to be impressive: competition wins, isolated revenue figures,
                  concrete human scale (80K+ businesses, 1,500+ SMBs).
                  Hard cap: 2 per resume. More than 2 reads as a highlights reel.

NO ARCHETYPE IS SUPERIOR

  Each archetype can score 10/10 when executed correctly for its own criteria.
  The scorer must evaluate each archetype against its own test — not a shared
  rubric that advantages Diagnostic.

  A scorer that makes Diagnostic easier to score 9-10 than Context-first has a
  calibration bug, not a quality insight. If Context-first consistently scores
  lower under the same rubric, the rubric needs archetype-specific tracks.

  There is no ceiling imposed by archetype choice. The ceiling comes from the
  story's earned detail potential evaluated against archetype-specific criteria:
    — Diagnostic ceiling: set by how genuinely non-obvious the insight is
    — Action-first ceiling: set by how specific and credentialing the method is
    — Context-first ceiling: set by how specific the scope and causal chain are
    — Impact-first ceiling: set by how directly attributable the outcome is

ARCHETYPE-SPECIFIC SCORING CRITERIA (what a 10 looks like per archetype)

  DIAGNOSTIC 10:
    • Insight is genuinely non-obvious — a non-participant would not write it
    • Corrected assumption passes detection test (someone outside would write
      a different specific phrase) AND removal test (bullet collapses without it)
    • Action follows causally and directly from the insight
    • Metric lands as the inevitable conclusion of the insight → action chain

  ACTION-FIRST 10:
    • Method or deliverable is specific enough that a non-participant would not
      guess it — names the actual design decision or technical artifact
    • Causal chain from action to outcome is complete — no invisible bridging step
    • The outcome is proportionate to and plausibly caused by the described action
    • Earned detail: a process artifact or decision trade-off that passes removal

  CONTEXT-FIRST 10:
    • Scope/goal opener is specific enough to be credentialing — not "worked on a
      platform" but "expanded marketplace supply by onboarding commercial fleet
      operators across Singapore and Bali"
    • Mechanism in second clause names a concrete deliverable or design decision,
      not a list of activities ("defined integration specs" ← too generic;
      "designed a standardized API contract and partner validation workflow" ← earned)
    • Causal bridge between mechanism and outcome is explicit — the intermediate
      step connecting what was done to the result must appear in the bullet
    • Outcome is proportionate to the described action and scope

  IMPACT-FIRST 10:
    • Outcome is directly attributable to Akshat's specific work — not a shared
      platform metric or team-level number
    • Supporting mechanism is visible and plausible — doesn't just state the result
    • Attribution holds under scrutiny — the described mechanism could plausibly
      produce the cited metric at the stated scale

WHY DIAGNOSTIC IS THE PLURALITY (for PM track)

  PM hiring's primary question: "does this person see things others miss, and do
  they translate that into the right product decision?" Diagnostic directly answers
  that. It's the plurality archetype because insight is PM's core differentiator
  from SWE (who executes) and PgM (who coordinates).

  This is a ROLE-SPECIFIC reason, not a universal quality ranking. For
  ops-execution non-PM roles, Action-first would be the plurality. For
  strategy/consulting, Diagnostic stays high because insight IS the consulting
  signal. The framework transfers across tracks; the weights shift.

  The 4-5 Diagnostic baseline does NOT mean the other archetypes are inferior —
  it means PM hiring prioritizes insight-first communication above the others.
  A resume with 10 Diagnostics fails just as badly as one with 0 — it reads as a
  thinker who never ships, and triggers monotony penalties.

"CAUGHT" AND OTHER DETECTION VERBS ARE DIAGNOSTIC

  Even though "Caught" has action energy, it encodes a detection insight:
  "I caught something wrong before it spread." That IS a diagnostic opener —
  it's insight-first (I saw it), not execution-first (I built something).
  Full list of diagnostic verbs: Identified / Diagnosed / Recognized / Surfaced /
  Found / Caught / Reframed / Synthesized [data → insight]
  Do NOT count these as action-first in the tally.
