## North-star identity

**Technical product builder who repeatedly finds the hidden system constraint behind a broken user workflow, turns it into a product decision, and earns trust through measurable execution.**

This is stronger and more coherent than pretending every engineering job was a conventional PM role. The technical background is the unfair advantage; the maxed profile adds explicit discovery, decision rights, commercial logic, experiments, adoption, and cross-functional leadership.

## Resume narrative

The one-page reference resume uses a strict evidence hierarchy:

- **FlairX first:** proves the transition is no longer only aspirational; it shows formal product ownership.
- **Gojek second:** shows marketplace scale, experiments, and technical-commercial judgment.
- **Hevo third:** shows enterprise product strategy and AI/reliability depth.
- **Intuit and Optum compressed:** preserve financial and regulated-trust proof without letting older SWE titles dominate.
- **Recruiting Engine as a project:** proves current hands-on product building and closes the “engineer trying to frame old work as PM” gap.

The reference intentionally uses counterfactual role labels such as “Product Engineer” or “Technical Product Lead.” Those labels are part of the experiment, not factual replacements for the real titles.

## Profile-maxing scoreboard

A story is not maxed because it sounds large. It is maxed only when all six proof loops exist:

1. **User:** a precise user and an observed pain.
2. **Decision:** a non-obvious choice with a rejected alternative.
3. **Mechanism:** a concrete product or system design.
4. **Ownership:** explicit “I” versus team boundaries.
5. **Outcome:** baseline, result, time window, measurement, and attribution.
6. **Reflection:** what changed in the operator after the result.

Each new story includes a truth-conversion plan. That plan is the practical output of this exercise: a list of work and evidence that would turn the idealized profile into a defensible one.
