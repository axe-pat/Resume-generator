# PX-16 - The Digital Beauty Authority

> **COUNTERFACTUAL REFERENCE - INVENTED/AMPLIFIED DETAILS - NOT FOR EXTERNAL USE**
>
> L'Oreal / USC Marshall consulting project · platform strategy / AI commerce · maxed lens: product-strategy consultant

## The product we finally build

A cross-brand beauty-discovery platform that begins with a consumer concern rather than a brand catalog. It combines an ingredient and claims knowledge layer, explainable routine recommendations across price tiers, expert/creator storefronts, cultural content, and a shared commerce layer. L'Oreal stops renting the first customer interaction from TikTok, retailers, and generic AI assistants and becomes the trusted starting point for beauty discovery.

## Fifteen-second version

L'Oreal's portfolio had reach but did not own discovery: consumers started with TikTok, ChatGPT, Sephora, or Amazon, then encountered individual brands. Our USC team proposed a $3.9M cross-brand platform that starts with the consumer's problem and turns L'Oreal's scientific authority into routines, content, and commerce. In the maxed version, I lead the product architecture, moat, and investment case, secure a Youth to the People pilot, and prove a 3.4% conversion lift plus a $6.80 basket increase before recommending broader rollout.

## Situation and stakes

Urban Decay, IT Cosmetics, and Youth to the People faced declining acquisition and engagement while Korean beauty brands changed the basis of competition toward ingredients, fast cultural cycles, and community validation. L'Oreal was present across retail and social channels but did not control the discovery layer or the first-party learning loop.

The obvious response - more brand campaigns - would intensify competition inside the same rented channels. The strategic opportunity was to use the portfolio itself as a platform advantage.

## The non-obvious insight

No adjacent player combines all four assets:

- retailers have distribution but must remain marketplace-neutral;
- social platforms optimize for virality, not scientific validity;
- marketplaces win logistics but lack institutional beauty authority;
- indie brands hold community trust but lack global R&D and portfolio breadth.

L'Oreal can create a differentiated discovery product if recommendations are transparent enough that “authority” does not become disguised self-preference.

## What I own in the maxed version

- Synthesize the consumer and competitive research into a product thesis: problem-first discovery, not a portfolio storefront.
- Define the five-layer product: solution-first discovery, cultural content, community/experiences, commerce, and trust/authority.
- Specify the AI routine builder: concern intake, ingredient/claim knowledge graph, contraindication and preference checks, explainable recommendation, alternatives across price tiers, and expert review for higher-risk claims.
- Design a neutrality contract: every recommendation shows why it fits, sponsored placement is labeled, and non-L'Oreal alternatives can appear when evidence supports them.
- Build the cross-brand unit economics: $3.9M shared investment weighted across three brands; acquisition, conversion, and basket-size drivers; 12-month payback scenario.
- Challenge the original all-at-once platform. Recommend a 12-week Youth to the People pilot because its ingredient-led audience provides the clearest learning environment.
- Define pilot metrics: qualified discovery completion, explanation engagement, routine add-to-cart, cross-brand attachment, conversion, average order value, repeat usage, and trust score.

## Product judgment and trade-offs

The central tension is commercial preference versus consumer trust. A platform that always recommends L'Oreal products is an advertisement, not an authority. The maxed strategy accepts some short-term leakage to non-portfolio products in exchange for credible discovery, first-party intent data, and long-term repeat use.

The $21M-$36M revenue case is a scenario, not an observed outcome. The maxed operator earns the next tranche through a contained pilot rather than presenting the model as certainty.
