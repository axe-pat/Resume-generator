Dear State Street Product Team,

During an enterprise trial at Hevo Data, I watched a deal stall not because our pipeline tool was slower than Fivetran's or weaker on connectors. It stalled because a data engineering lead could not tell, at 9 a.m. on a Monday, whether Friday's batch job had completed cleanly. The feature gap was never the problem. The visibility gap was.

That observation reshaped how I thought about product. I had been treating Job Monitoring as a debugging surface, a utility for engineers to consult after something broke. When I reframed it as the primary proof of enterprise readiness, the product roadmap changed. I worked with engineering to make job status, failure boundaries, and recovery events first-class information, not log-file archaeology. The result was a monitoring dashboard that became our best sales artifact: a client could see exactly when a pipeline failed, why, and what recovered. Mean time to resolution dropped roughly 40%, and eight enterprise customers who had previously passed on Hevo signed within 90 days of the change. Visibility is a product decision. Treating it as an afterthought is a business cost.

State Street's OTC Alpha platform sits at precisely this tension. Institutional clients expect front-office speed; regulators expect back-office auditability; and the delivery teams in between are being asked to satisfy both simultaneously. The moment where that pressure concentrates is in the trade lifecycle pipeline itself: when a commitment slips, a dependency shifts, or planned scope diverges from delivered scope, the question is whether product leadership sees it in time to act or learns about it in a review after the fact. The work of this role is building the reporting and tracking infrastructure that makes the former, not the latter, the default.

In the first month, I would map every open epic on the OTC Alpha Jira board to its stated acceptance criteria and delivery owner, surface the three epics where planned versus delivered scope has drifted furthest, and bring a one-page bottleneck summary to product leadership. The question I would want that summary to answer is the same one the data engineering lead was asking on a Monday morning: not what was supposed to happen, but what actually did.

Sincerely,
Akshat Pathak
akshatpathakrocks@gmail.com
