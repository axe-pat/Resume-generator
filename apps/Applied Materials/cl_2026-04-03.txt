Dear Applied Materials Strategy Team,

The complaint came in as an ops problem. Peak-hour shortages in Singapore and Bali: not enough drivers, dispatch queues backing up, conversion dropping. The operations team wanted headcount. The engineering team wanted better routing. Neither answer was wrong. But when I pulled the behavioral data and ran interviews with dispatch coordinators and fleet partners, something else showed up. The real ceiling wasn't drivers or algorithms. It was that the platform had no interface for external supply to join at all.

That reframe changed the entire work. Instead of optimizing within the existing system, I defined the requirements for a partner integration API that could open Gojek's supply layer to external fleets. I aligned engineering and operations around the new scope, ran the research to validate which fleet segments would actually convert, and drove rollout across Singapore and Bali. Active supply rose 18% and pickup ETAs dropped 1.5 minutes within the quarter. The constraint was architectural, not operational. Nobody had looked at the market signal clearly enough to see it.

That same diagnostic instinct shaped how I approached enterprise sales at Hevo, where I found that Fortune 500 data engineering teams weren't evaluating pipeline speed. They were evaluating auditability. The strategic pivot that followed ; batch-first transactional execution over streaming ; was built on a customer segment insight, not a feature request.



Applied Materials is navigating a version of that same inflection. The business is moving from one-time capital equipment transactions toward recurring, intelligence-driven service relationships through AGS, even as gate-all-around and backside power delivery create a new adoption cycle that foundry and IDM customers are reading differently. The strategic question is not whether that transition is happening. It is which customer segments move first, and which equipment categories face substitution pressure from Lam or ASML before the recurring revenue layer matures enough to carry the margin.

That is precisely the kind of ambiguous signal I want to spend 90 days sharpening. I would start by segmenting foundry versus IDM adoption timelines for gate-all-around, map which equipment categories sit in the highest substitution risk window, and deliver a competitive positioning brief the Strategy team can actually act on. The diagnostic question I would want answered by the end: is the AGS opportunity a retention play on existing customers, or an acquisition surface for the next technology node?

Sincerely,
Akshat Pathak
akshatpathakrocks@gmail.com
