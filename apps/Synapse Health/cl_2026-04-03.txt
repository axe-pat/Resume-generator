Dear ofi (Olam Food Ingredients) Strategy Team,

A production system failing silently is, in some ways, more dangerous than one that crashes. When 1,500+ businesses on QuickBooks Payroll began experiencing billing mismatches, the issue had already propagated across three systems before anyone had a clear picture of the failure boundary. I led the cross-functional response: coordinating Engineering, Product, and Support simultaneously while we traced the failure to its source rather than its symptoms. What that incident taught me has stayed with me since. Every operational breakdown, whether in code or on a production floor, has an upstream cause that the downstream noise is hiding.

At Intuit, I diagnosed that the root cause wasn't a bug but a missing reconciliation layer across billing services that had never been designed to share state. I built the financial case to pause feature delivery in favor of fixing that seam, designed the cross-system framework that resolved the data mismatches, and presented the business impact to senior leadership. In parallel, at Hevo Data, I turned a monitoring tool that engineers used purely for debugging into a structured audit surface that showed data platform owners exactly when pipelines failed, why, and what recovered. Reducing MTTR by approximately 40% was a side effect. The real outcome was that evaluators could see failure boundaries clearly for the first time, and signed contracts followed. Visibility is a product decision. It is also an operations decision.



What draws me to this role at ofi is the unit economics of the Nuts business in Hughson. Private label margins leave no room for yield loss to hide. When manhours-per-pound drift or throughput falls short, the cost lands immediately on margin with no pricing lever to absorb it. That is a diagnostic environment I understand: the constraint is real, the data is available, and the question is whether anyone has mapped where the loss is actually occurring versus where it first becomes visible.

In the first 90 days, I would map the end-to-end processing line against current manhours-per-pound and OEE data to isolate the two or three points where yield loss is highest, then run structured root cause sessions with production leads to determine whether those losses are equipment-, workflow-, or scheduling-driven. The question I would want answered before recommending any process change is the same one that mattered at Intuit: is the problem where it appears, or is it upstream?

Sincerely,
Akshat Pathak
akshatpathakrocks@gmail.com
