Dear Snowflake Product Team,

Enterprise data teams have a question they ask before they ask anything else: "If something goes wrong with my data, will I know?" Not eventually. Not after a support ticket. Right away, with enough context to act. At Hevo, competing against Fivetran for Fortune 500 trials, I watched evaluators open our demo, skip past the pipeline speed benchmarks, and go straight to the logs. That question was the real evaluation criteria. The roadmap hadn't caught up to it yet.

So I changed the roadmap. Job Monitoring had been scoped as a debugging tool, buried in the product for engineers who already knew where to look. I recognized it as the artifact enterprise evaluators actually needed: a first-class audit surface showing data platform owners exactly when a pipeline failed, why, and what recovered. Repositioning it as a product surface meant rethinking the information hierarchy, the alert taxonomy, and the failure-state UX from the ground up. The result reduced mean time to resolution roughly 40% and became the centerpiece of every enterprise trial. Alongside that, I led the architectural pivot to batch-first transactional execution, trading latency for verifiable failure boundaries. Within 90 days, eight enterprise customers who had previously passed on Hevo signed. A product that fails loudly is easier to sell than one that fails silently.

Snowflake's original architectural bet on separating compute from storage created an enterprise moat. The next version of that bet looks harder. As Cortex and the agentic enterprise narrative expand the surface area of what Snowflake owns, the trust question shifts: it is no longer just "what happened to my data in the warehouse" but "what did my AI agent do with it, and can I prove it to a regulator?" That question lives precisely in the governance and lineage layer, where Databricks' lakehouse model and Apache Iceberg's open-table format are applying the most competitive pressure. That is the product surface I want to work on.

In the first 90 days, I would map where Cortex AI feature adoption drops off relative to core warehouse usage for mid-market data engineering teams, specifically to distinguish whether the blocker is lineage opacity or pricing model complexity. The answer to that question determines whether the governance layer is a retention tool or a conversion tool. I learned at Hevo that those are not the same product.

Sincerely,
Akshat Pathak
akshatpathakrocks@gmail.com
