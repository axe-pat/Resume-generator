Dear Shop ONline New York Product Team,

In an enterprise sales trial at Hevo Data, a data engineering lead stopped our demo before we reached the features slide. He didn't want to see ingestion speed. He wanted to know what happened when a pipeline failed at 2 a.m. and his team woke up to silence. That question reordered everything I thought I understood about what a product backlog should protect.

The feature he was asking about existed, loosely, as a debugging tool buried three menus deep. I recognized it as something else: the artifact that would determine whether enterprise evaluators felt safe enough to sign. I pulled it out of the backlog, shaped it into a first-class monitoring surface, and worked with engineering to define exactly what information a pipeline owner needed to see at failure, at recovery, and at every step between. The work meant sitting with sales calls, translating anxiety into acceptance criteria, and prioritizing a feature that didn't show up in any usage metric yet. Visibility is a product decision. Once Job Monitoring shipped as a product surface rather than a diagnostic afterthought, trials started converting. The feature that closes deals isn't always the one already on the roadmap. It's the one a stakeholder describes as a fear.



The same translation problem exists in e-commerce, just in a different register. A user who abandons a checkout isn't always leaving because the price is wrong. Sometimes the gap is between what a page communicates and what a buyer needs to feel confident. Identifying that gap requires the same skill: sitting between a stakeholder's intuition and an engineering team's backlog, and turning an observed friction point into a story that a sprint can actually resolve.

In the first 90 days, I'd map the end-to-end purchase flow, pull session data or user feedback to locate the top drop-off points, and bring a prioritized backlog of experiments to the team with evidence behind each one. The question I'd want to answer first: what does a user believe they're missing right before they leave?

Sincerely,
Akshat Pathak
akshatpathakrocks@gmail.com
