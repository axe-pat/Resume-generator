Dear Actian Product Team,

During an enterprise trial at Hevo, a data engineering lead from a Fortune 500 company told me the pipeline was fast. Then he said they couldn't sign. The failure logs were opaque. If something broke in production, his team would find out from a downstream analyst, not from the system. Speed wasn't the problem. Accountability was. That conversation reoriented everything I thought I knew about what enterprise customers were actually buying.

The fix wasn't in the architecture. It was in how the product represented itself to the people who had to stake their credibility on it. Job Monitoring had existed at Hevo as a debugging tool, buried in a developer view. I recognized it as the feature that enterprise evaluators needed to feel safe: a first-class audit surface showing data platform owners exactly when pipelines failed, why, and what recovered. I reframed it as a product surface, shaped the requirements around auditability rather than throughput, and worked with engineering to make failure visible, labeled, and recoverable. MTTR dropped roughly 40%. Within 90 days, eight enterprise customers who had previously passed on us signed. Our best sales demo wasn't a features slide. It was a monitoring dashboard. The feature that closes enterprise deals isn't always on the roadmap. Sometimes it's already built, waiting to be treated like a product.



What draws me to this role is a specific architectural tension in how the AI Analyst maintains "one version of the truth" when the underlying metrics definitions are themselves contested between Sales and Product. The Steward Inbox is where that tension becomes visible: every query failure is either a prompt precision issue or a signal that the Semantic Layer's business logic is under-specified for a real workflow. Getting that diagnosis right is what separates a pilot that scales from one that stalls at the showcase.

In the first 90 days, I'd map every Steward Inbox failure back to a gap in the Metrics Definitions Glossary, not just to fix the query, but to surface which business logic is under-specified before the pilot gets presented as a model for the rest of the organization. The question I'd want answered by day 90 is the same one that unlocked the Hevo story: what does the system look like to the person who has to stake their credibility on it?

Sincerely,
Akshat Pathak
akshatpathakrocks@gmail.com
