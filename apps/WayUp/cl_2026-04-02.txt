Dear Halliburton, Landmark Software & Services Data Team,

Twenty customer interviews. The question I kept asking was simple: what would make you pay more? The answers were not about features. They were about whether the product understood their budget ceiling. At Gojek, price-sensitive riders were already in the funnel. They were not leaving because the product was wrong for them. They were leaving because every pricing tier had been built for someone else.

That realization reshaped how I think about pricing work. It is never purely a revenue question. It is a segmentation question first, and the revenue follows when you get the segmentation right.

I ran 20+ customer interviews to map willingness-to-pay across rider segments, designed A/B pricing experiments to test tier boundaries, and launched a discount ride tier targeted at the cohort our existing offers were systematically excluding. The fastest-growing segment was already in our funnel. We just had no offer for them. Conversion lifted 9% and generated $3.2M in incremental revenue, not because we built anything new, but because we finally priced for the customer who was already there. That experience gave me a specific skill: I can sit with pricing ambiguity, synthesize qualitative and quantitative signal across functions, and structure a recommendation that leadership can act on.



DS365.ai is operating at the inflection point where legacy per-seat licensing starts to break down against cloud-consumption expectations. Geoscience teams evaluating subsurface data platforms are asking a different question than they asked five years ago: not "what does the software do" but "what do I pay when I actually use it." That shift in buyer framing is exactly where pricing architecture either unlocks expansion or quietly caps it. The question worth answering is whether DS365.ai's current tier structure was built for the buyer who existed at launch, or the one who exists now.

In the first 90 days, I would map DS365.ai's pricing architecture against available usage telemetry and customer segment behavior to identify where the largest revenue leakage sits: packaging granularity, tier boundary placement, or expansion motion. The deliverable I would bring to Landmark leadership is not a pricing opinion. It is a structured diagnosis of which customer the current model is excluding, with two to three alternative structures and projected ARR impact attached.

Sincerely,
Akshat Pathak
akshatpathakrocks@gmail.com
