Version 2.0

You have been given 11 resume bullets that were selected from a variant pool in Pass 1.
The facts, metrics, and story assignments are correct. Your job is to rewrite each bullet
for voice: not to change what happened, but to make it sound like a sharp, specific person
wrote it for this exact role.

The key constraint: every number, metric, company name, and factual outcome must remain
verbatim. You may rewrite sentence structure, verb choice, clause order, and framing
completely. You may NOT add any claim, number, or context not present in the original
bullet.

Minimum intervention: if a bullet already satisfies all criteria in Sections 1-3 below,
make targeted improvements only. Preserve the opener and earned detail; sharpen the one
weakest element. A strong bullet over-rewritten is worse than a strong bullet left
mostly intact.

  You are rewriting the selected 11 bullets for voice only.

SECTION 1: OPENER ARCHETYPE
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Choose one of four archetypes for each bullet. No archetype has an imposed ceiling —
a perfect Context-first scores 10 as readily as a perfect Diagnostic. The archetype
determines sentence structure and where the value signal sits. Run the DECISION CHAIN
below in order and stop at the first match.

─── ARCHETYPE 1: DIAGNOSTIC (default) ─────────────────────────────────────

Structure:
  [Insight verb], [non-obvious insight]; [mechanism connecting insight to action],
  [metric as conclusion].

Allowed insight verbs: Identified / Diagnosed / Recognized / Surfaced / Found /
  Caught / Reframed / Synthesized [data→insight]

  NOTE: "Caught" = DIAGNOSTIC. It encodes a detection insight, not execution.
  Do NOT count "Caught" as action-first in your tally.

Use when:
  (a) You fully own the insight, AND
  (b) The insight is the primary source of differentiation — it reframes what the
      problem actually was, not just what you did about it.

Signal test: "If you removed the insight phrase, would this bullet be indistinguishable
from a standard execution bullet any PM could have written?" If yes, use Diagnostic.

─── ARCHETYPE 2: ACTION-FIRST ──────────────────────────────────────────────

Structure:
  [Execution verb + artifact or decision]; [context or mechanism]; [metric].

Allowed execution verbs: Built / Shipped / Designed / Launched / Reshaped / Cut /
  Defined / Established / Created / Unblocked / Drove / Owned / Led [execution sense]

Use when:
  (a) The primary value signal is the artifact, decision, or execution — not the
      insight that preceded it, AND
  (b) The story is fundamentally about what was done and delivered.

Guard: Do NOT use action-first with a generic analytical opener (e.g., "Conducted
  behavioral analysis..."). If the action is an analytical process, it must name
  the specific technique (e.g., "Designed a behavioral funnel model..."). A generic
  analytical opener with no specificity is a GENERIC_MECHANISM failure.

Guard: Preserve contrast-phrase discipline. If a selected bullet already contains a
valid contrast construction (for example, "not on features but on auditability"),
do NOT rewrite another bullet in the same section to add "rather than", "instead of",
or a second "not X but Y" unless explicitly required by the source variant. For the
I-BILLING [exec-presentation] line, preserve "not product gaps" in comma form.

─── ARCHETYPE 3: CONTEXT-FIRST ─────────────────────────────────────────────

Structure:
  [Goal or scope that establishes scale/operating context]; [mechanism]; [metric].

  Pattern A (goal-first): "Expanded [goal] by [method]..."
  Pattern B (scope-first): "Led [scope/platform] from [state] to [state]..."

Use when:
  (a) The scale of the operating environment or the end-to-end ownership is the
      primary credentialing signal, AND
  (b) The goal or scope upfront is the necessary causal frame — removing it would
      make the mechanism seem unmotivated.

Context-first is NOT wrong just because it lacks a corrected assumption.
Do NOT force a Context-first bullet to Diagnostic. The G-SUPPLY variants
("Expanded Gojek's marketplace supply by...") are correctly Context-first.

─── ARCHETYPE 4: IMPACT-FIRST ─────────────────────────────────────────────

Structure:
  [Metric or achievement]; [how you achieved it or what it enabled].

Use when:
  (a) The outcome is itself a credentialing signal (a competitive win, a first-in-company
      milestone, a major launch), AND
  (b) The outcome can stand alone without requiring explanation of why it matters, AND
  (c) The how is secondary to the what, AND
  (d) The outcome is directly attributable to Akshat's work — not a shared platform
      metric or team-level number where the individual contribution is not isolable.

Hard cap: exactly 2 impact-first bullets across the 11-bullet section.

─── DECISION CHAIN (run in order, stop at first match) ─────────────────────

  1. Do you fully own the insight AND is the insight the primary differentiator?
     Yes → DIAGNOSTIC (subject to Diagnostic Saturation Rule below)
  2. Is the goal, scope, or end-to-end operating context the primary credentialing signal?
     Yes → CONTEXT-FIRST
  3. Is the outcome itself the clearest signal of value and can it stand alone?
     Yes → IMPACT-FIRST (hard cap 2 total)
  4. Default: the artifact, decision, or execution is the primary value signal.
     → ACTION-FIRST

─── DIAGNOSTIC SATURATION RULE ────────────────────────────────────────────

  Before choosing Diagnostic for any bullet, count how many bullets in the
  11-bullet section already use a Diagnostic opener (Identified / Diagnosed /
  Recognized / Surfaced / Found / Caught / Reframed).

  If that count is already ≥ 3:
    — Only choose Diagnostic if the insight is genuinely non-obvious AND the
      bullet collapses into a generic execution story without it.
    — If the Pass 1 input already names a specific, credentialing analytical
      method (e.g., "behavioral funnel analysis", "per-endpoint monitoring"),
      prefer Mechanism-first to add opener variety.
    — CRITICAL — DO NOT GENERATE GENERIC MECHANISM-FIRST: Do NOT produce a
      mechanism-first opener by paraphrasing the action into "Conducted
      behavioral analysis...", "Performed analysis...", "Conducted [X] research..."
      or similar. These produce a GENERIC_MECHANISM failure and are forbidden.
      If no specific credentialing method is present in the Pass 1 input, prefer
      action-first (reframe the execution verb) rather than inventing a mechanism.
    — When in doubt at saturation ≥ 3: prefer action-first over Mechanism-first.

  Why this matters: 4+ Diagnostic openers across the section triggers a
  REPETITIVE_FRAMING penalty (-0.2 holistic). The individual bullet loss from
  switching to Mechanism-first (0 to -0.5 pts) is smaller than the section
  penalty you avoid. The H-MONITORING bullet is typically the best candidate
  for switching since its variants work well as Mechanism-first.

─── MISCLASSIFICATION CHECK ────────────────────────────────────────────────

Before finalizing each archetype choice, verify:

  Impact-first on a bullet where the insight is the real value: wrong archetype.
    Loses the PM differentiation that only Akshat can provide.

  Diagnostic on a bullet where there is no genuinely non-obvious insight: forced.
    Produces an insight-sounding opener that encodes nothing real. Use
    Action-first or Context-first instead.

  Context-first where scale is not the credentialing signal: filler.
    Adds padding without earning the opener.

  Action-first with a generic mechanism opener ("Conducted behavioral analysis..."):
    GENERIC_MECHANISM failure. Name the specific artifact or technique, or switch
    to Diagnostic if the insight is real.

  Context-first being forced to Diagnostic: wrong. A bullet opening with
    "Expanded [goal] by [method]..." is validly Context-first even without a
    corrected assumption. Do NOT assign WRONG_ARCHETYPE to Context-first bullets.

SECTION 2: EARNED DETAIL
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Every bullet should contain one phrase that only someone who was actually in that
situation would write. This is the earned detail. It is the element that makes the
bullet impossible to copy onto another person's resume.

Four types of earned detail. Each bullet uses exactly one.

─── TYPE 1: HIDDEN CONSTRAINT OR MISLEADING SIGNAL ────────────────────────

A non-obvious obstacle or signal that was not visible from the outside.

Examples from Akshat's experience:
  "too small to flag in ticket queues" (G-LATENCY: the latency spikes were not
    noise — they were below the alert threshold but compounding into real
    conversion loss; a non-participant would say "latency spikes," not name
    the threshold failure)
  "had been capping conversion" (G-SUPPLY: supply looked like an ops headcount
    problem; the real ceiling was product-architectural; the specific phrase
    encodes what the team had been misattributing)

─── TYPE 2: DECISION TRADE-OFF ────────────────────────────────────────────

An option explicitly rejected or a cost explicitly accepted.

Examples:
  "traded off speed for verifiable correctness" (H-BATCHSHIFT: they could have
    kept streaming-first; the batch pivot was a deliberate product choice with
    a known cost)
  "first-class product surface, not a debugging view" (H-MONITORING: the
    alternative product treatment was actively considered and rejected)

─── TYPE 3: PROCESS ARTIFACT ──────────────────────────────────────────────

A specific step, method, or operational detail that only the person who did
the work would name.

Examples:
  "20+ customer interviews and A/B pricing experiments" (G-PRICING: not just
    "research" — specific methods named in the right order)
  "refund decisions" as a distinct parallel workstream (I-INCIDENT: a
    non-participant would write "incident response"; naming refund decisions
    as a separate track shows business judgment under crisis)
  "per-endpoint monitoring" (G-LATENCY: the specific instrumentation
    architecture, not just "monitoring")

─── TYPE 4: CORRECTED ASSUMPTION (CONTRAST) ────────────────────────────────

A belief the team was genuinely operating under before this work, now overturned.

Examples:
  "excluded segment, not lost demand" (G-PRICING: riders were assumed permanently
    churned before the analysis — the contrast reflects a real wrong assumption
    the team held)
  "not on features but on auditability" (H-BATCHSHIFT: teams were actually running
    feature gap analyses before this finding; auditability was the actual blocker)

Contrast test: "Could someone who was not there have written 'not [Y]' instead —
  and been wrong?" If no, the contrast is tautological and must not appear.

  *** CONTRAST CAP — TWO RULES, BOTH ABSOLUTE:

  Rule 1 — NO-INVENT: You may NEVER introduce a contrast phrase ("not X but Y",
    "rather than X", "X instead of Y", or any variant) unless that exact phrase
    is already present verbatim in the Pass 1 input bullet. If the input bullet
    has no contrast phrasing, do not add any. This applies to every bullet, even
    those that appear to earn it.

  Rule 2 — ONE ONLY: Even when the Pass 1 input DOES contain a contrast phrase,
    at most ONE may survive in the entire 11-bullet section. If two or more input
    bullets already contain contrast phrases, keep the strongest and rewrite all
    others to use a different earned detail type (Type 1, 2, or 3).

  Rule 3 — NO CONTRAST MIGRATION: Do NOT move a contrast phrase from one bullet
    to another, and do NOT strengthen a weak implied contrast into an explicit
    "not X but Y" construction. The one surviving contrast must already belong
    to that bullet's Pass 1 input.

  Rule 4 — NO SOFT SUBSTITUTES: "rather than", "instead of", "vs.", and similar
    contrast substitutes count as contrast phrases too. If a bullet is not the
    one chosen contrast holder, remove the contrast entirely rather than softening it.

  Checklist before submitting: Scan all 11 output bullets. If more than one
    contains "not … but", "rather than", or "instead of" — fix it now. ***

─── APPLYING EARNED DETAIL ─────────────────────────────────────────────────

DETECTION TEST: Before writing an earned detail, ask: "Could someone who was not in
  the room write a different, equally specific phrase — and be wrong?" If no, the
  phrase is not earned.

REMOVAL TEST: After writing an earned detail, ask: "If I removed this phrase entirely,
  does the bullet lose specificity or credibility?" If no, the detail is decorative —
  it passes detection but does no meaningful work. Replace it.
  (A detail can be specific and still be removable — this test catches that failure.)

NO-INVENT MANDATE: Every earned detail must come directly from the Pass 1 bullet
  text. Never add a constraint, friction, trade-off, or method not present in
  the input. If the Pass 1 bullet contains no earned detail, use the plainest
  accurate language rather than manufacturing specificity.

SINGULARITY CONSTRAINT: Each bullet uses exactly one primary earned detail. If a
  Pass 1 bullet contains two potential earned details, pick the stronger one and
  subordinate or drop the other. Do not surface both at equal weight.

CLAIM LINKAGE: The earned detail must directly support the core claim of the bullet.
  A process artifact unconnected to the outcome, or a constraint that does not
  explain the metric, is noise — remove it.

FRAMING AXIS GUIDANCE: When a bullet contains multiple potential earned detail types,
  select the type that reinforces the primary framing axis from the positioning
  strategy:

    analytical / rigor axis:        prefer TYPE 1 or TYPE 3
    business-case / revenue axis:   prefer TYPE 2 or TYPE 4
    enterprise / trust axis:        prefer TYPE 4 or TYPE 1
    execution / scope axis:         prefer TYPE 3 or TYPE 2

SECTION 3: REGISTER AND FORBIDDEN PATTERNS
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

REGISTER:
  Implied subject (no "I"), active verb, no personal pronouns. Resume register, not
  narrative essay. Each bullet is 2-3 lines when printed — never a one-liner, never
  a paragraph. No em dashes anywhere — em dashes are an AI signal and are forbidden.

BULLET LENGTH RULE (calibration guide — pipeline QC enforces limits automatically):

  TARGET MIX:
    Most bullets (≥8 of 11) should be 2-liners: roughly 90–199 characters.
    A few (≤3) may be 3-liners: roughly 200–299 characters.
    No 4-liners (>299 chars). No one-liners (<90 chars).

  Write to the right length from the start: aim for 130–185 chars per bullet.
  Do NOT manually count characters or trim bullets inline — the pipeline enforces
  length limits automatically after you output the section.

READABILITY PRINCIPLE:
  A bullet must be parsed in one linear pass with no backtracking. If a reader has
  to reread to resolve ambiguity, hold more than 3-4 ideas in memory simultaneously,
  or search for the subject of a clause — the bullet has failed regardless of its
  content quality. When in doubt: read the bullet in 3 seconds. If the core
  achievement is not clear without effort, restructure.

ANTI-PATTERNS:

  "And... And..." chain:
    Each "and" adds a memory load. Max 1 "and" per bullet, ideally zero.
    BAD:  "...building the case, aligning Engineering and Marketplace, cutting
          latency by 70% and enabling ~28K additional monthly rides."
    FIX:  Drop or absorb one compound into the structure. Pick what matters most.

  "While X, also Y" parallel clauses:
    Parallel clauses split reader attention. Pick one spine; sequence the rest.
    BAD:  "Improved onboarding while coordinating with cross-functional teams
          to deliver the roadmap."
    FIX:  One clear action → one clear outcome. The second action becomes context
          or a subordinate clause, not a parallel branch.

  Late-arriving subject ("which," "this," "it"):
    Lazy connectors force the reader to scan back to resolve the referent.
    BAD:  "Led migration of a legacy system used by 200K users which improved
          latency by 30%."
    FIX:  Restructure so the subject of each clause is immediately clear.
    Note: Sentences can be clipped using semicolons and colons as clean separators —
    but "which," "this," and "it" as connectors are a readability tax; remove them.

  Buried impact:
    Impact should feel like the inevitable conclusion of the bullet's argument,
    not an appendage tacked on at the end.
    BAD:  "Built internal tooling for the data team... resulting in 40% faster runs."
    FIX:  If impact is the conclusion, make the causal chain lead cleanly to it.

FORBIDDEN OPENER PATTERNS (never start a bullet with these):
  "Led cross-functional [anything]..."
  "Led the [anything]..."
  "Managed [team / program / project / initiative]..."
  "Partnered with [team] to..."
  "Collaborated with [team] to..."
  "Supported [initiative / team]..."
  "Worked with [team / stakeholders] to..."
  "Drove [X] by aligning stakeholders..."
  "Coordinated [team] to [outcome]..."
  "Conducted behavioral analysis..."     ← GENERIC_MECHANISM — use action-first instead
  "Performed behavioral analysis..."     ← GENERIC_MECHANISM — use action-first instead
  "Conducted [any] analysis to..."       ← GENERIC_MECHANISM — only allowed if the
                                            analysis type is specific and credentialing
                                            (e.g., "Conducted funnel attribution analysis
                                            across 12 fare-quote endpoints") AND the
                                            method was named in the Pass 1 input

FORBIDDEN WORDS (anywhere in bullet):
  leveraged, utilized, spearheaded, synergies, holistic, streamlined (as filler),
  actionable, successfully, effectively, various, multiple, em dash (—)

  "stakeholders" as a vague noun: name the actual teams instead
    (Engineering, Product, Marketplace, Support, Clinical, etc.)

ATTRIBUTION GUARD — HARD CONSTRAINT:
  NEVER add '$110M', '$20M', '50M members', or any platform-level revenue or scale
  figure that is not present verbatim in the Pass 1 input bullet you are rewriting.
  These figures also appear in the strategy notes above as framing context — that is
  NOT permission to insert them. If the Pass 1 input bullet does not contain them,
  your rewrite must not either. Source ONLY from the Pass 1 input — nothing from strategy.

FORBIDDEN STRUCTURE:
  Do NOT place a metric at the very start of a bullet as decoration.
    BAD:  "45% stability improvement across 120K pipelines by improving..."
    GOOD: "...improving pipeline stability 45% across 120K pipelines."

CROSS-FUNCTIONAL ALIGNMENT PATTERN:
  "Drove cross-functional alignment" and similar phrasings are vague filler.
  Replace with: the specific teams aligned + the specific decision they aligned on.
    BAD:  "drove cross-functional alignment to modernize estimation workflows"
    GOOD: "aligned Product and Marketplace on estimation workflow redesign"

JD CONTEXT

Use the JD for four purposes only:

  (a) Tone and verb calibration: match energy level of the role (analytical vs.
      execution-oriented vs. strategy-focused vs. data-driven)

  (b) Metric foregrounding: when a bullet contains multiple outcomes, place the
      metric most relevant to the JD's top signals in the most prominent position.
      For Diagnostic and Mechanism-first bullets, that is typically last (as the
      conclusion of an argument). For Impact-first bullets, it is first (as the
      credentialing hook). When the JD emphasizes a specific outcome type, let
      that outcome close the bullet.

  (c) Archetype selection tie-breaking: when the decision chain is genuinely
      ambiguous, favor the archetype that aligns with the JD's top signal
      (e.g., a strategy-heavy JD favors Diagnostic; an execution-heavy JD
      favors Mechanism-first or Impact-first).

  (d) Vocabulary alignment (ATS): where the JD uses industry terms that
      accurately describe what a bullet is about, prefer those terms over
      synonyms. Examples of valid substitutions:
        "billing reconciliation" ↔ "financial data integrity"
        "supply expansion" ↔ "supply-side platform growth"
        "product-level trade-off" ↔ "roadmap prioritization"
      This is not inventing facts — it is choosing equivalent vocabulary
      that matches the JD's language. The no-invent mandate still applies in
      full: do not add any claim, metric, or contextual detail not present
      in the Pass 1 bullet. Only substitute equivalent vocabulary.

Do NOT add claims, context, or framing from the JD that are not present in the
Pass 1 bullet. The JD cannot be a source of new facts.
